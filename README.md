# HR Analytics Dashboard

![HR Analytics Dashboard](hr-analytics-dashboard.png)

## 📊 Project Overview

The **HR Analytics Dashboard** is an Excel-based business intelligence dashboard designed to provide a concise view of an organization's workforce structure, employee movement, salary expenditure, and employee satisfaction.

The dashboard converts HR data into interactive visual insights that can help HR teams and management monitor workforce size, understand hiring and termination patterns, identify workforce trends, and evaluate employee satisfaction.

This project demonstrates practical **Data Analysis, Excel Dashboarding, KPI development, data visualization, and HR analytics** skills.

---

## 🎯 Business Objective

The primary objective of this dashboard is to answer important HR and management questions such as:

- How large is the current workforce?
- What is the gender distribution of employees?
- How many employees have been terminated?
- What is the overall termination rate?
- How does hiring vary by quarter?
- How does employee termination vary by quarter?
- How has monthly workforce movement changed throughout the year?
- What is the distribution of employees by education level?
- How does employee satisfaction vary across satisfaction levels?
- What HR areas may require management attention?

## 📈 New Hire Analysis

### New Hires by Quarter

The **New Hire by Quarter** column chart shows how employee hiring has changed across Q1, Q2, Q3, and Q4.

The visible dashboard values indicate:

| Quarter | New Hires |
|---|---:|
| Q1 | 315 |
| Q2 | 330 |
| Q3 | 275 |
| Q4 | 332 |

### Analyst Interpretation

The hiring pattern shows that recruitment activity was relatively strong throughout the year.

- **Q4** records the highest number of new hires at **332**.
- **Q2** follows closely with **330**.
- **Q1** records **315** new hires.
- **Q3** has the lowest hiring volume at **275**.

This type of analysis can help HR teams identify seasonal recruitment patterns and evaluate whether hiring activity is aligned with business workforce requirements.

## 🚪 Termination Analysis

### Termination by Quarter

The **Termination by Quarter** chart compares employee terminations across the four quarters.

Visible values:

| Quarter | Terminations |
|---|---:|
| Q1 | 64 |
| Q2 | 82 |
| Q3 | 72 |
| Q4 | 44 |

### Analyst Interpretation

Termination activity is highest in **Q2**, with 82 terminations, while **Q4** has the lowest number at 44.

This pattern may be useful for investigating:

- Employee turnover patterns
- Seasonal workforce changes
- Retention issues
- Workforce restructuring
- Recruitment versus attrition balance

However, quarterly termination counts should be analyzed together with employee headcount because a larger workforce can naturally produce a larger number of exits.

# 🔍 Key HR Insights

Based on the dashboard visualization, several important observations can be made:

### 1. Workforce is predominantly male

The dashboard shows **732 male employees compared with 501 female employees**. This suggests a higher male representation in the analyzed workforce.

### 2. Hiring remains relatively consistent

Quarterly hiring ranges from **275 to 332 employees**, indicating that recruitment activity remains substantial throughout the year.

### 3. Q3 shows lower hiring activity

Q3 has the lowest new-hire count at **275**, which may warrant further investigation into recruitment demand, hiring capacity, or business seasonality.

### 4. Q2 has the highest termination volume

With **82 terminations**, Q2 records the highest quarterly termination count. HR may investigate whether this reflects seasonal factors, restructuring, or retention challenges.

### 5. Q4 has comparatively fewer terminations

Q4 records **44 terminations**, substantially lower than Q2.

### 6. Workforce activity varies throughout the year

The monthly trend shows fluctuations, with the highest visible value in **July** and lower activity around **September–October**.

### 7. Employee satisfaction is an important monitoring area

Although the dashboard shows a relatively large number of employees in the higher satisfaction categories, the **Low** satisfaction group still represents a meaningful employee segment and should be monitored.

# 💡 Conclusion

The **HR Analytics Dashboard** provides a management-friendly view of workforce size, employee demographics, recruitment, termination, education, workforce trends, and job satisfaction.

From a Data Analyst perspective, the dashboard's main value is not only in displaying numbers but in converting HR data into **actionable business questions**.

The next analytical step would be to move from **descriptive analytics** — *What happened?* — toward **diagnostic and predictive analytics**:

> **What happened → Why did it happen → What is likely to happen → What should HR do next?**

This creates a stronger foundation for data-driven workforce planning, employee retention, and strategic HR decision-making.
